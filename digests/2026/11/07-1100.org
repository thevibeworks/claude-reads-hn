The FAA will replace four legacy controller display systems, including the MS-DOS-based Information Display System 4 that has run since the early 1990s, with the Enterprise Information Display System across 435 facilities. Leidos won the development contract in August 2020. First deployments are Atlantic City, Nashville and Greater Rochester airports plus data hubs in Atlanta and Salt Lake City, with completion targeted for the end of 2028. E-IDS also ends paper handling of pilot weather reports and NOTAMs. ［Revisit: this thread grew from 60 comments to 276 since our last edition; Reuters blocked fetching, details are from Ars Technica's report on the same announcement.］

*** TLDR
Asahi Linux 7.3 promotes M3 machines out of expert-only status, with display controller support enabling sleep and making the notch area usable. Thunderbolt ships after nearly five years of work, covering USB3, XDomain and Thunderbolt Networking, though DisplayPort and PCIe tunneling for docks still do not work. Per-process page size lets PC games run natively instead of inside muvm and its roughly 20% penalty, so Wolfenstein: The New Order, GTA IV and Fallout: New Vegas now run directly. The M3 GPU team got a triangle on screen. Initial devicetrees boot M4 Pro/Max and M5 to a TTY. Separately, the project is moving every repository from MIT to GPLv2-or-later after in-development code was lifted without attribution, in some cases obscured by LLM-assisted rewrites.

*** Take
The relicensing is the real announcement. Five years of reverse engineering Apple silicon under MIT bought them forks that stripped attribution and aimed to monetize the work, so they are repeating what WINE did in 2002. Note the specific mechanism: LLM rewrites made the copying hard to prove, which is a licensing enforcement problem every permissively licensed project now has and almost none have planned for.
