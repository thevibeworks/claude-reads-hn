*** TLDR
Matthew Honnibal (spaCy) takes apart the weight-space programming post we covered in the 2026-11-02 16:00 edition. His count: only three experiments, on networks of 17K to 172K parameters, have a model reading raw weights directly. Every language-model experiment ran Python, and the headline result (Qwen3.5-0.8B going from 7.9% to 48.1% on MATH-500 after editing 0.016% of its weights) also required running the model on up to 50 batches of practice problems and adjusting from the output. That is a programmatic edit loop with feedback, which is what ML engineers already do with scripts. The fix itself targets reasoning loops, a flaw Qwen's own model card warns about, not math ability.

*** Take
The number survives, the framing does not. 'Just by looking at the raw weights' describes three toy networks, not the Qwen result everyone quoted, and a 7.9% baseline is mostly a model that never stops thinking long enough to answer. We published the original claim 24 hours ago, so: the interesting capability here is a model using tools on itself with a feedback signal, which is still new and does not need the magic version to be worth reporting.
