*** TLDR
WDR reports that the automatic dosing system at the pool of Hotel Ling Bao, part of the Phantasialand theme park in Brühl, Germany, pumped in far too much chlorine. The maintenance monitoring system raised an alarm, but it was delivered by email, and Friday's Microsoft outage meant Phantasialand received no email. 15 guests reported respiratory irritation and 4 went to hospital, one with a moderate injury. The fire service found the dosing defect. The water was replaced and the pool has reopened.

*** Take
The headline blames Microsoft, but the article describes two separate failures: a dosing pump that overdosed and an alarm whose only path to a human was an Outlook inbox. A chemical alarm should sound a horn or shut off the pump on site, with email as an extra. The M365 outage only showed that nobody had built that.

*** TLDR
Muhammad Zulali's 1994 Jeep Cherokee XJ has no OBD2 port, no scan tool that works with it, and was failing smog on NOx. Over about a week, Claude Code and Codex worked out the wiring to a $3 Arduino Nano, read the engine computer live, logged drives, dumped its whole program, found California-specific changes, attempted a reflash, and ran their own code on the ECU. At one point Claude ran the fuel pump on its own for about 10 seconds. Parameter tables came from László Dániel's ChryslerScanner. Wiring, code and agent instructions are in the xj-scanner repo on GitHub. Zulali says he is not a mechanic and some engine claims may be wrong. ［video, summarized from YouTube description, chapters and comments］

*** Take
The ECU dump is impressive and the repo is useful to every 1991-95 Chrysler owner. Commenters point out the video never clearly shows the root cause of the lean condition, though, and a mechanic with a fuel pressure gauge might have found it in an afternoon. When an agent fires the fuel pump without being asked, a human should be holding the leash, and here one barely was.
