*** TLDR
Microsoft 365 went down globally for hours on 12 November, taking out Outlook, Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive, Copilot 365, Defender and Power BI. Microsoft said a large number of servers became unexpectedly unavailable, pushing traffic onto what remained, and pointed at a component handling health checks that may have misfired after a recent configuration update. It said this was not an attack. Recovery came in stages: an alternative health check was validated on a subset of servers and then applied more widely, followed by an automated process to detect and restart unresponsive servers. Microsoft's own status page buckled under load, so HN readers subscribed to its RSS feed instead.

*** Take
The fix Microsoft shipped mid-incident, automatic detection and restart of unresponsive servers, is the thing that should have existed before the incident. A health check that marks working servers dead, with no limit on how many it can condemn at once, is a single point of failure sitting on top of a system designed to have none. The status page failing while its RSS feed kept working is the other lesson: the simplest path out was the one nobody was maintaining.

*** TLDR
Rust 1.100.0 shipped on 12 November. The never type, written !, is stable after ten years and five failed stabilization attempts; it is the canonical uninhabited type, carried by expressions like return, break and process::exit, and it coerces to any other type. Also stable: the Allocator API for custom allocators, a DropGuard API, and conjuring zero-sized types. On the Cargo side, unused dependencies are now reported as warnings by default with the offending Cargo.toml line, cache collisions are reduced, cargo-install reuses an existing lockfile, and min-publish-age adds dependency cooldowns: the resolver ignores versions published more recently than a configured age unless they are already in Cargo.lock. Commenters listed stabilized APIs the blog post skipped, including bool::toggle, Box::map, str::trim_prefix, str::trim_suffix and Path::trim_prefix.

*** Take
min-publish-age is the most consequential item in the release and it is four bullets down the page. It answers the supply chain attack that keeps working: a maintainer account is compromised, a malicious patch version goes up, and every CI run in the world pulls it within minutes. A cooldown window costs you nothing except freshness you did not need and removes the entire window of exposure. The never type gets the headline because ten years and five attempts is a better story, but the cooldown is the line that changes your risk profile today.
