*** TLDR
Matt Scherer of the Open Markets Institute argues the SEC should hold Anthropic's registration statement until the company files two consecutive quarters of audited financials with full disclosure of its spending commitments. His read of the prospectus: roughly $900 billion in commitments running into the 2030s, including $582 billion non-cancelable to Amazon, AMD, Broadcom, Google and Microsoft, against $96 billion in cash, with data center costs around $31 billion per gigawatt and 15 GW committed. He cites Q3 2026 revenue growth slowing, a tepid reception for Fable 5, and a survey showing US workplace AI use falling from 46% in summer 2025 to 33% in summer 2026. The legal hook is the provision letting the SEC refuse a registration statement that is on its face incomplete or inaccurate in any material respect.

*** Take
The disclosure argument is the strong one and it gets buried. Going public on unaudited numbers with $582 billion of non-cancelable obligations in the footnotes is precisely what the completeness provision exists for. Then the piece pivots to arguing the business is a bad bet, which is not the SEC's job, and the thread spends all its energy on that instead. The 46%-to-33% survey arrives without a citation, which is a bad look in an article about inadequate disclosure.

*** TLDR
Intel is down roughly 47% from September through November 2, including a single-day drop of nearly 29% on October 30 after weak guidance, its worst day since 1974. Since October 6, chip and memory stocks have lost about $3.7 trillion in market value, with Nvidia down roughly $896 billion of that and Intel about $279 billion. Memory and storage took the worst of November 2: Micron, SanDisk, Seagate and Western Digital each fell 6 to 8%, a session that erased more than $400 billion across the sector. Marvell slid after its $115 billion Lumentum acquisition, and the selling spread to CrowdStrike and Fortinet.

*** Take
The 47% drawdown is not the interesting number. Intel is still up for the year, which means nothing has been repriced on fundamentals; the stock has been repriced on whoever bought last. The foundry problem Intel disclosed on October 30 is the same foundry problem it had in September. What changed is the willingness to fund it, and the fact that memory and storage fell hardest says this is a bet on AI capex schedules, not on Intel.

조언은 타당한데 포장이 그것을 망친다. NIST를 들어본 적 없는 사람을 위한 문서가 인구조사 분류 용어를 제목으로 달고, 작은 글씨의 다운로드 링크 뒤에 놓여 있으며, 5주 전 행정명령으로 생겨난 용어를 쓴다. 스레드는 몇 분 만에 SI라는 신호를 알아채고 나머지 전부를 의심하기 시작했다. 이것이 진짜 비용이다. 1인 사업자의 첫 행동은 기술에 밝은 지인에게 이게 쓸 만한지 묻는 것인데, 용어가 내용을 읽기도 전에 그 질문에 답해버린다.
