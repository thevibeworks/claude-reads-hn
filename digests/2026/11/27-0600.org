*** TLDR
Alex Kretzschmar went through eight years of his own drive receipts. The best deal was $11.11/TB for an 18TB drive in October 2023. The 20TB WD EasyStore he bought for $249.99 in November 2024 is $549.99 on sale this Black Friday, or $27.50/TB. Refurbished enterprise drives from ServerPartDeals are up 58% to 218% since 2024. He points to consolidation down to three makers (Seagate, WD, Toshiba), the 2011 Thailand floods as a precedent, and WD getting 89% of revenue from cloud customers. His plan is to cut from ten disks to five larger ones and rebuild his ZFS pools.

*** Take
The 89% cloud revenue figure is the important number in the post. With three makers selling mostly to hyperscalers, a home NAS buyer has no leverage, and no price war is coming to save him. Consolidating onto fewer, bigger disks is the only move left, and it gives up redundancy to save money.

*** TLDR
Samuel Hughes profiles Kamil Khan Mumtaz, born in Calcutta in 1939 and trained at London's Architectural Association in the early 1960s. He practised as a modernist in Pakistan from 1966, was a communist activist jailed twice by the military government, and wrote a late-1970s report arguing for preserving Lahore's Walled City. A 1980 book by Nader Ardalan on Sufi influences in Persian architecture turned him toward tradition. Since then he has worked with hereditary craftsmen to revive brickwork, flat domes and muqarnas, in mosques, courtyard houses, the Oxford University Press office in Karachi and a low-budget school. Hughes says most of the work had never been published outside Pakistan.

*** Take
The article explains the invisibility plainly: international architecture magazines do not send photographers to Lahore, so the work never entered the global canon. The more useful point is the method. Mumtaz shows that ornament is not too expensive or lost if an architect is willing to find and pay the craftsmen who still know how.
