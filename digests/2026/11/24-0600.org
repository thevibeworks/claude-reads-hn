** Stalwart is now a drop-in replacement for Microsoft Exchange :email:self-hosting:microsoft:exchange:open-source:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50620349

*** TLDR
Stalwart 1.2.0 adds MAPI over HTTP, Exchange Web Services, ActiveSync and Autodiscover. Classic Outlook for Windows (2016 and later), Outlook for Mac, Apple Mail, and iOS and Android Exchange clients can now connect to it as if it were an Exchange server. The Exchange module ships in the AGPL Community edition behind a feature flag. Not supported: the new Outlook for Windows, Outlook mobile, public folders, archive mailboxes, device management, and NTLM or Kerberos sign-in. Migration uses Stalwart's Vandelay tool, which can import from on-prem Exchange over EWS and from Microsoft 365 over Graph. Also new: mail rules compiled to Sieve, read receipts, room and equipment booking, and a native FoundationDB client.

*** Take
An open source implementation of MAPI and NSPI is real engineering work. The thread shows that the remaining barriers are not protocols. They are German GoBD archiving rules, Outlook.com blocking self-hosted senders by /24, and the admin who gets blamed when mail stops. The compatibility also rests on classic Outlook, and Microsoft is moving customers to the new Outlook, which Stalwart cannot serve.

*** TLDR
Governor Kathy Hochul signed S.9348A/A.10411A. It bars manufacturers, dealers and their agents from charging subscriptions for car features that use hardware already installed at purchase or lease and that need no ongoing cost or support from the seller. Heated seats and remote start are the cited examples. The bill text exempts navigation updates, infotainment, satellite radio, in-car Wi-Fi, telematics, roadside assistance, software-dependent driver assistance and automation, and services that rely on cellular data. Sellers also cannot charge for software updates that fix safety defects. The penalty is up to $250 per point of sale per violation. It takes effect February 27, 2027.

*** Take
The governor's press release lists hands-free driving and enhanced navigation among the features this protects, and the bill exempts both. What it covers is heated seats, remote start and similar switches. A $250 fine per sale is smaller than many of the subscriptions it targets, and any feature that a manufacturer routes through a cellular connection qualifies for the exemption.
