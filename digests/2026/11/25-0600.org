*** TLDR
An OpenAI preprint, credited only to "OpenAI" with no individual authors, claims the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4 over every field. For every n, any arithmetic circuit multiplying two n×n matrices needs at least ceil(n^2.25/2) multiplication or division gates. The new part is the lower bound: if the exponent were smaller, you could compress tensor powers of trivalent networks enough to beat a known lower bound for testing whether entries in several lists are distinct. The matching upper bound came from an earlier OpenAI paper and from Amanov, van den Berg and Zuiddam. It is one of four papers posted November 24 that together claim 8 problems from an LLM-ranked list of 500 open problems.

*** Take
The ω = 2 conjecture is dead, and the paper killing it has 109 pages and no human author. Nobody is on record to answer a referee. Also, nothing gets faster: the result is a floor, and the circuits that reach n^2.25+ε are asymptotic constructions nobody will ever run.

*** TLDR
Scott Aaronson writes about the November 23 proofs of Valiant's VP≠VNP conjecture, released the same day by Anthropic and OpenAI. Anthropic gets a circuit size lower bound of exp(Ω(log^(4/3) n)) for the permanent, OpenAI exp(Ω(log^(5/4) n)), against a known upper bound around 2^n. Both use compression followed by diagonalization, which may dodge the natural proofs barrier, and both ship Lean formalizations. Aaronson had dropped his confidence in P≠NP from 97% to 95% after the Exponential Time Hypothesis refutation and moved it back to 96% after this. He says the past week looks like the start of an AI singularity, and in the comments he says he has heard many rumors that a lab is sitting on a proof that factoring is in P.

*** Take
The content here is a complexity theorist publicly recalibrating in real time, with numbers attached. The useful signal is not the wine: it is that Aaronson's objection list has shrunk to "I will listen to experts with technical issues, not to people who say this cannot be happening." The factoring rumor is the part worth watching, because it would reprice every RSA deployment still running.

*** TLDR
On 120 Hz ProMotion iPhones, WebKit caps requestAnimationFrame and pointer events at 60 Hz for web pages unless the user digs into a Safari developer setting. The author measured scroll events arriving as rarely as 5 times per second during momentum scrolling. The workaround puts an invisible scrollable element over the area you want to track: scrolling is handled by the compositor, so the page can poll scrollTop far more often and treat it as trackpad input, keeping native momentum and bounce. Reading WebKit source showed Safari only flushes the scroll position without waiting when a rendering update is already queued, so an empty requestAnimationFrame loop is needed to raise sample rate. Output goes through OffscreenCanvas from a worker, which renders at 120 FPS without waiting for the main thread. Measured 118 FPS against a steady 60 for pointer events. None of it is documented API.

*** Take
This is good reverse engineering of a limit that should not exist: Apple ships 120 Hz hardware and gives native apps the frame rate while holding the web at half. The honest caveat is in the post itself, that the whole stack rests on current WebKit behavior Apple never promised. Note also how many commenters could not tell the demos apart, which is worth knowing before you build on an undocumented path.
