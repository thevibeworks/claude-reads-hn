*** TLDR
A senior C#/.NET/AWS engineer in the Washington DC area with 15 years of experience quit a 1,000-person insurance-tech company in January 2025 after a tip that layoffs were coming, and has not found a software job since. Savings cover four to five more months. They tutor math and are about to start as a pizzeria manager. They ask how to explain the gap and what else pays well. Replies fall into six camps: contact every former colleague, build a visible project, consult or freelance, reframe or lie about the gap, move to trades, IT, public sector or defense work near DC, or accept that the market has changed for good.

*** Take
The most consistent evidence in the thread is that almost nobody reports getting hired from cold applications, and nearly every success story went through someone who already knew the candidate. The popular advice to invent a two-year startup aims at the resume filter, which by the thread's own accounts is the channel that is not producing hires. Messaging former managers and coworkers costs nothing and matches what actually worked for the people replying.

*** TLDR
Mickey Petersen, author of Mastering Emacs, announced DreamCoder after about 15 months of work. It is an AI coding-agent harness that runs as a browser app on macOS, Windows, Linux and Docker, and Petersen says it is not an Emacs package. It has the standard pieces (background sub-agents, MCP servers, skills, hooks, terminals next to chats, custom agents) plus a tiling window manager that uses a constraint solver to size panes, virtual-desktop workspaces, and a minimap that shows which agents are working or idle. Keyboard commands borrow from Emacs packages Avy, Embark and Helm. It imports configs from Pi, OpenCode and Codex and targets local open-weight models such as Qwen 3.8 27B. Most of the post is a history of Lisp and AI. No source code or pricing is listed; a commenter says it requires account registration and a EULA.

*** Take
Petersen's bet is that the bottleneck in agent work is now the person supervising several agents at once, so Petersen built a window manager instead of another prompt loop. That bet is reasonable and few harnesses take it seriously. But the post argues from taste and offers no benchmarks, and a closed, registration-gated release asks an audience that chose Emacs for its openness to take the quality on trust.

***** Title
Sir Brian May verleiht Heras Asteroiden eine neue Dimension
***** TLDR
Die ESA hat stereoskopische 3D-Ansichten des Asteroiden Didymos veröffentlicht, erstellt von Brian May, Queen-Gitarrist, Astrophysiker und Mitglied des Hera-Wissenschaftsteams. Das erste Paar nutzt zwei Hera-Aufnahmen vom 6. November aus 29,7 km Entfernung mit 2,8 m pro Pixel. Eine zweite Version, mit Claudia Manzoni erstellt, kombiniert benachbarte Bilder der Asteroid Framing Camera, die kurz nacheinander aufgenommen wurden. In 3D wirkt der Äquatorgrat von Didymos scharf und glatt im Vergleich zur felsübersäten Oberfläche anderswo. Die Bilder funktionieren mit Rot-Blau-Brille, im Kreuz- oder Parallelblick oder mit Mays eigenem OWL-Stereoskop. Heras erste Nahaufnahmen des Didymos-Systems werden am 25. November 2026 von 19:45 bis 20:30 MEZ in einem Livestream gezeigt.
***** Take
Die Schlagzeile verkauft den Rockstar, aber Stereopaare sind bei dieser Mission echte Arbeitsdaten: Mays frühere Paare des Marsmonds Deimos aus Heras Vorbeiflug 2025 flossen in eine Simulationsstudie zur Geschichte dieses Mondes ein. Heras Aufgabe ist zu messen, was der DART-Einschlag der NASA 2022 beim Didymos-Mond Dimorphos bewirkt hat, und Tiefeninformationen über die Oberflächenform gehören dazu. Die Nahaufnahmen vom 25. November sind die, auf die man achten sollte.
