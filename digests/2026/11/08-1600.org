** Llama-modchip: 284B DeepSeek on 64GB RAM, no GPU :ai:llm:performance:quantization:cpu:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50411961
:URL:      https://github.com/synchopate/llama-modchip

:POINTS:   174

:BY:       joshcsimmons

*** TLDR
A llama.cpp fork that runs large models on CPU only. It introduces two custom block-64 quantization formats, Fractal-64 at 3.75 bits per weight and Fractal-MXFP4 at 4.25 bpw, using BF16 scales over Gray-coded tensors. DeepSeek-V4-Flash 284B drops from 76.87 GiB to 56.56 GiB on disk and 57.61 GiB peak RAM, helped by an analytical second-order SwiGLU-H2 compression that cuts 20.31 GiB of activations. Memory access is tiled across four levels, from AVX-512 ZMM registers down to L3. On a 16-core Zen 5 with 64GB it measures 44.4 tok/s prefill and 9.1 tok/s generation, rising to 11.5 to 14.7 tok/s with a speculative decoding drafter the README reports at 96.4 percent acceptance. 16GB runs 30B models; Zen 4/5, Apple Silicon and ARM64 are supported.

*** Take
The number everyone repeats is 284B on 64GB, and the number that decides whether this is useful is 3.75 bits per weight. Published quantization curves put the knee around 4 bits, so this sits just under it and the README offers benchmark tables instead of perplexity against the unquantized model. antirez running it fast enough for a coding agent on 128GB is the strongest evidence here, and it is one person's report. The tiling and the drafter are real engineering; the quality claim is the part still owed a measurement.
