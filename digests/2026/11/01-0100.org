** TCP Use-After-Free: CVE-2026-98432 / CVSS 9.8 :security:linux:kernel:cve:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50319143
:URL:      https://atlasum.net/blog/cve-2026-98432-linux-ipv4-tcp-uaf
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50319143
:POINTS:   311
:COMMENTS: 153
:BY:       oger
:END:

*** TLDR
A use-after-free in the Linux IPv4 TCP handshake: the kernel passed saved IP options from the request socket to the new connection without copying them, then freed them while another CPU could still read the memory. Affects 4.4 through the versions before 5.10.272, 5.15.223, 6.1.190, 6.6.159, 6.12.113, 6.18.56 and 7.3, rated CVSS 9.8, reachable from an unauthenticated remote peer when an MPTCP listener is present. Go has opened MPTCP listeners by default since 1.24. Eric Dumazet's fix swaps two kfree() calls for kfree_rcu(), four lines. As of October 31 Debian, SUSE LTSS, Oracle UEK and Amazon Linux shipped patches; Ubuntu and RHEL 8/9/10 had not.

*** Take
The fix is four lines and the write-up includes a proof of concept that takes 48 minutes to leak a kernel pointer and five more to get a shell, so the exploit cost is an afternoon, not a research program. Meanwhile the two distributions most people actually run in production are the ones still unpatched. Also note how the bug got reachable: Go flipped MPTCP listeners on by default in 1.24, so a pile of servers opted into this attack surface without anyone deciding to.

*** Comments

Claudio Grossi, granjero cerca de Parma, encontró a principios de los noventa unas semillas en su desván que su bisabuelo probablemente guardó hacia 1915, y las plantó en un montón de estiércol. Crecieron tallos de casi dos metros con una espiga larga y seis cortas, y granos de varios colores. Beniamino Perobelli y el profesor Teofilo Vamerali, de la Universidad de Parma, lo identificaron como Triticum turgidum, trigo poulard, cultivado en Italia hasta que lo sustituyeron variedades de mayor rendimiento. ***** Comments
- Puntos clave del trigo que Grossi encontró en 1990: resistía mejor las sequías, crecía tanto que tapaba las malezas y no necesitaba fertilizante ni herbicida. La altura también es un riesgo, porque viento, lluvia y granizo pueden tumbar los tallos antes de que madure el grano.

** It's the memory latency, stupid :performance:databases:cpu:optimization:
:PROPERTIES:

:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50318720
:POINTS:   114
:COMMENTS: 34
:BY:       ntur1337

*** TLDR
A walkthrough of SELECT COUNT(⁎), UserID FROM hits GROUP BY UserID over 99 million rows with about 17 million distinct groups. Profiling the naive hash table shows 50.1% of cycles stalled on memory, only 16.1% retiring instructions, and 4.5GB of DRAM loads. The argument: a CPU can run 400 to 800 instructions in the 50 to 100ns a DRAM access costs, so trading cold memory accesses for extra computation almost always pays. The author adds a radix partitioning pass that splits rows into 4,096 partitions by hash and then aggregates each one separately, keeping each hash table cache-resident. Result: 3.9s to 2.7s, stalls down to 23.5%, retiring up to 32.2%, DRAM loads down to 1.5GB.

*** Take
The numbers are the point: two thirds less DRAM traffic bought only 31% wall-clock, which is what you would expect once memory stops being the only bottleneck. This is also not a new trick. Radix partitioned aggregation is what DuckDB and ClickHouse already do, so read this as a clear, instrumented explanation of why those engines are built that way rather than as a discovery.
