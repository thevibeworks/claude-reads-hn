** Unkernel: Real Linux kernel, compiled to WebAssembly :webassembly:linux:browser:emulation:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50674218
:URL:      https://unkernel.dev
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50674218

:BY:       nreece

*** TLDR
Pooya Parsa (pi0) built unkernel: Linux 7.x compiled to a single wasm module with a real MMU, using an arch/wasm port with page tables. The kernel itself runs as compiled wasm; user programs are unmodified aarch64 binaries running on kwemu, a Zig CPU emulator linked into the kernel, with a JIT for hot code. It boots Alpine or Debian in the browser after a ~10 MB download, or in a terminal via npx unkernel. The docs claim /init in 70 ms, fork+exit+wait in about 60 µs (70 µs native, 1.2 ms under qemu), and systemd booting Ubuntu 26.10. It needs JSPI (Chrome 137+, Firefox 153+, Safari 27+), browser networking goes through a Wisp proxy, and the docs admit CPU-heavy programs are well behind native. MIT licensed, sponsored by Vercel.

*** Take
The design choice that matters is which half gets emulated. Kernel paths like fork, syscalls and page faults run as compiled wasm, so they are near native; only user code is emulated, so CPU-bound work stays slow. That explains both the commenters amazed by the snappy desktop and the one who found it too slow for real work. This is also a repost from five days ago, and Tabernetes and unlab are already built on it.

** Google's DeepCrossAttention achieves comparable model quality up to 3x faster :ai:machine-learning:google:transformers:
:PROPERTIES:

*** TLDR
A Google Research blog post on DeepCrossAttention (DCA), from an ICML 2025 paper (arXiv 2502.06785). Standard residual connections add each layer's output to the stream with equal weight; DCA adds a small per-layer module that learns, per token, how to mix earlier layers' outputs, plus cross-attention across depth instead of across tokens. The post claims the same quality up to 3x faster in its intro and up to 5x fewer training steps on C4 and LM1B in its body. A 30-layer DCA model matched a 42-layer baseline on LM1B, at under 0.2% extra parameters. Retrofitting DCA onto a pretrained model and training briefly cut LM1B perplexity by 0.19, versus 0.02 for plain continued training. It costs extra memory bandwidth. The speedup is in training, not inference, and Gemini is not mentioned.

*** Take
The headline figure shifts three times in one post (3x, 5x, 30-40% fewer parameters), the results are on C4 and LM1B, and no model sizes are given. Read it as a promising small-scale result, not as the explanation for Gemini Neon. The thread's best question is why Google blogged an 18-month-old paper now; the most plausible answer offered is that labs publish once an edge is no longer worth hiding. Most of the 159 comments compare Gemini Neon with Claude subscriptions instead of discussing residual connections.
