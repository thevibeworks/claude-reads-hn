** The AI cost of JavaScript frameworks :javascript:ai:benchmarks:webdev:

*** TLDR
Nolan Lawson had Claude Opus 5.5 agents build the same Pokemon app in 12 frameworks (Angular, Astro, Gracile+Lit, Hono+htmx, Next.js, Nuxt, Qwik, React Router, SolidStart, SvelteKit, TanStack Start, vanilla Vite) and measured what each run cost. Median spend ranged from $2.67 for Hono+htmx to $4.97 for Angular. Astro was fastest at 8.2 minutes against Angular's 17.2, Nuxt used the fewest tokens at 3.85M against Angular's 9.76M, and Hono+htmx produced the least code at 1,370 lines. Angular failed twice, so only three of its runs counted. Lawson notes the app is content-heavy with little interactivity and that Lighthouse scoring favors multi-page architectures over SPAs.

*** Take
The spread is real but small: under three dollars separates best from worst, which is less than the agent's own hourly cost to a human sitting there watching it. The number that should bother people is 9.76 million tokens for a Pokemon list page. Lawson's own stated point, that you can decide with evidence instead of vibes in the agentic era, is the durable part; the leaderboard is the part that will get screenshotted out of context.

*** TLDR
Mordechai Guri's RAMBO paper shows malware already running on an air-gapped machine can modulate memory bus activity so the RAM emits radio signals, which an attacker picks up with a software-defined radio and decodes. It can carry files, keystrokes, biometrics and encryption keys at up to 1000 bits per second. Range trades against speed: at the slow rate the signal holds at 7 meters, at the medium rate about 4.5 meters with a 3 to 4 percent bit error rate, and less at the fast rate. Accepted at the Nordic Conference on Secure IT Systems in 2023 and posted to arXiv in September 2024.

*** Take
Every one of these papers starts from the assumption that already-running malware is on the air-gapped box, which is the hard part of the attack the paper does not solve. Granted that, you get 100-odd usable bits per second from 7 meters with line of sight, and a USB stick or a camera pointed at the screen beats it outright. The useful finding is not the exploit but the emission: cases with big acrylic windows and plastic laptop shells radiate in a way full metal enclosures did not, which is a procurement question for anyone who actually runs air-gapped machines.

Arecibo's instrument platform hung over the 305-meter dish on steel cables, each terminated in a socket where the strands are splayed and set in molten zinc. The National Academies failure analysis concluded the zinc slowly deformed and let the cable strands pull out of their sockets, which it calls the first documented case of long-term zinc-induced creep failure despite more than a century of use of this termination. Visual inspections after Hurricane Maria in 2017 found large and progressive pullouts, and the platform came down in December 2020. The report floats, as an untested hypothesis, that the telescope's own transmitted radio power accelerated the creep through long-term low-current electroplasticity.

The part engineers treat as the reliable end of the system is the part that failed, and it failed through a mechanism nobody had a century of data for.
