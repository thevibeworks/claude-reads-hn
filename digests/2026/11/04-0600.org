*** TLDR
Simon Willison ran the 17GB Q4_K_M quantization of Qwen 3.8 27B in LM Studio on his laptop and handed it the raw weights of four small networks trained to play Atari games, with no code execution or tools allowed. ** Safari introduces Adaptive Dark Mode in 27.3 :safari:webkit:css:accessibility:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50358562
:URL:      https://webkit.org/blog/18541/introducing-adaptive-dark-mode/
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50358562
:POINTS:   375
:COMMENTS: 206
:BY:       paddingtone
:END:

*** TLDR
Safari 27.3 can darken sites that never declared dark mode support. WebKit applies a color matrix that inverts luminosity while preserving hue, aiming for the WCAG 4.5:1 contrast ratio, converts light CSS gradients, inspects SVGs and adjusts their colors, and inverts simple black-and-white bitmap logos based on a pixel histogram. Photos and canvas content are left alone, and effects like masks and mix-blend-mode may be disabled to avoid breakage. Pages that declare color-scheme: light dark are skipped entirely, light only opts out, and JavaScript still reads the original colors so a site cannot detect that the transform ran. WebKit's stated motivation: nearly 40% of page loads come from users in dark mode while only 10% of loads support it.

*** Take
That 40-versus-10 gap is WebKit admitting the opt-in approach failed and the browser will now do it for you. Blocking detection is the detail that matters most, because an API exposing whether your transform is active would be one more fingerprinting bit for nothing.
