*** TLDR
［NYT paywalled; based on the Harvard CEPR study and its discussion in the thread］ The New York Times covers a Harvard CEPR working paper on schools that adopted lockable phone pouches, measured with NWEA MAP Growth tests. The estimated gain is about 0.01 standard deviations in the adoption year (95% CI roughly 0.0003 to 0.017), with no further gain the next year. A commenter found that without the authors' trimming of extreme outliers, which was not preregistered, the estimate falls to 0.006 SD and is no longer significant. Co-author Tom Kane replied that the dropped scores looked like data errors.

*** Take
A 0.01 SD effect that depends on an outlier rule chosen after the fact does not support the headline. The parents in the thread make a better case for bans: kids talk to each other at lunch again. That argument doesn't need test scores to back it.
