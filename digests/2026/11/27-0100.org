*** TLDR
Google made eight Workspace MCP servers generally available on Nov 24 and changed its API terms. From Feb 1, 2027, any tool that is or is driven by an AI agent must reach Gmail, Drive, Calendar and the rest through those servers, and agents calling REST directly drop to Standard quotas. Agent status is self-declared with an OAuth checkbox. The Gmail server can search, read, draft, label and trash, but cannot send, reply, forward or edit a draft. Those actions are partner-only: Anthropic's Claude connector has them, independent developers with the same scopes do not. Google can cap retention of derived data such as embeddings at 30 days, and training or building a competing product is banned. The free tier is 5,000 requests per day, and a Gmail search counts as 10. Paid billing starts April 1, 2027.

*** Take
Google's argument for one gateway, where admins can approve and audit agent actions, is a real defense against prompt injection in email agents. But the public server already lets an agent trash mail and only blocks sending, and MCP has an elicitation feature for asking the user to confirm. So the line follows who has a deal with Google, not what is dangerous. The whole rule also depends on agents honestly ticking a box. The likely result is agents driving the Gmail web UI through a browser, which is worse for users and for Google.

*** TLDR
Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, 52, co-founder and CTO of the Florida company ransomwarehelp.com, was indicted for wire fraud in Rhode Island. The company advertised "proprietary tools and algorithms" for 100% data recovery and said it never paid ransoms. In an FBI sting, agents posed as both a victim and a ransomware group. Palacio allegedly asked the fake group for its lowest price (0.2 BTC, about $13,000), quoted the victim $13,260 for decryption plus a $2,750 diagnostic fee, then talked the group down to about $6,000. Krebs also describes a 2024 LockBit case where the firm quoted a small accounting firm $438,880 and negotiated with LockBit in secret, asking the gang to keep it from the victim. The case is part of a wider DOJ push: MonsterCloud's CEO allegedly paid $8 million in ransoms while billing customers $19 million.

*** Take
Much of the thread asks whether this counts as fraud if the customer gets their files back. It does: customers paid extra specifically for a firm that promised not to pay criminals, and the invoices described forensic work that did not happen. The practical lesson for small businesses is that a recovery firm claiming it can break modern ransomware encryption is either paying the ransom or lying. According to the indictment, this one did both.

***** Title
AMD-spezifischer Fork von llama.cpp
***** TLDR
Der Autor (HN-Einreicher julianlam) empfiehlt llama-cpp-rdna-boosts vom GitHub-Nutzer stew675: eine kleine Sammlung AMD-spezifischer Patches, die auf festgelegte llama.cpp-Commits neu angewendet und als ROCm-Container-Image ausgeliefert werden. Auf einer AMD R9700 im eGPU-Gehäuse stieg mit einer Q6-Quantisierung eines Qwen3.8-27B-Modells und aktiviertem MTP der Prefill von etwa 700 auf 1.300 Token pro Sekunde und die Generierung von 35 auf 55. Der Autor grenzt es von neueren, per Vibe Coding gebauten Inferenz-Engines wie Strata ab, die Tempo gewinnen, indem sie das Modellverhalten ändern (etwa Experten bevorzugen, die schon im VRAM liegen), und gab einen AMD-vLLM-Fork auf, weil der Build für einen einzelnen Rechner zu aufwendig war. Der Patch-Code selbst sei LLM-generiert und etwas undurchsichtig.
***** Take
Das Verkaufsargument ist Zurückhaltung: Beschleunigungen, die nicht ändern, was das Modell berechnet, in einem Diff, der klein genug zum Prüfen ist. Genau deshalb ist es ein Fork. llama.cpp unterstützt eine lange Liste von Backends und gehört laut Kommentaren über die Hugging-Face-Übernahme inzwischen NVIDIA. AMD-only-Patches, die andere Hardware verschlechtern könnten, sind upstream schwer durchzusetzen. Der Thread stellt die richtige Frage: Fast niemand, der diese Forks benchmarkt, misst die Ausgabequalität, nur Token pro Sekunde.
