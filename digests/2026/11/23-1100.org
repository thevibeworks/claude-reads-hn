** XNU Linux: Full OS X GUI Userspace on Linux :macos:linux:kernel:apple:reverse-engineering:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50607261
:URL:      https://github.com/AFK-surf/xnulinux
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50607261
:POINTS:   350
:COMMENTS: 163
:BY:       mmastrac
:END:

*** TLDR
xnulinux builds XNU, the open-source macOS kernel, from Apple's sources and loads it as a kernel module (xnu.ko) inside a stock arm64 Linux kernel. A second GPL module (fki.ko) lends it CPUs, memory, timers, files, network and GPU. With a stock Ubuntu kernel, insmod boots a macOS 27 disk to the desktop with loginwindow, Dock, Finder, Safari and Chrome, in 31 to 48 seconds. Metal runs over Vulkan through Mesa's Venus driver. It currently runs in an arm64 Linux VM on Apple Silicon, needs your own macOS install (no Apple binaries ship), and relies on patched AMFI and relaxed SIP.

*** Take
This is not a way to run macOS on a cheap PC. It needs Apple Silicon, an Apple-made system disk, and security features switched off. What it does show is that the Mach-on-Linux layering works well enough to survive a 30-minute Safari stress run. Apple's lawyers will care less about the kernel module than about the kexts and disk it pulls from your Mac.

*** TLDR
The author checks whether the 17 million lines of Lean in the recent Riemann hypothesis proof is as large as it sounds. Of 259 famous theorems with Lean proofs in Mathlib, nearly all depend on 100k to 1.2m lines, and all of Mathlib is 2.4m. Of 938 packages on the Reservoir index, the median is about 4k lines and only three are bigger than 17m: an AI-built finite simple groups classification (88m, unfinished), LeanBridge (36m, 98% script-generated database checks) and Anthropic's Fermat's Last Theorem proof (27m, about 450 times the size of Kevin Buzzard's human FLT project).

*** Take
The chart answers its own question: machine proofs are long because nothing pushes them to be short. A commenter reports a community effort has already cut it to 13m lines, which suggests a lot of it is duplicated or inlined library material. Length does not affect whether Lean accepts the proof. It does decide whether a mathematician learns anything from reading it.
