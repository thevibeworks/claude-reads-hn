*** TLDR
Mark Cuban says Meta knowingly lets AI deepfake ads using his likeness run, calls Facebook "the worst of the worst," and says the company knows elderly users are being defrauded. He points at Reuters reporting on internal Meta documents: the company projected roughly $16 billion, about 10% of 2024 revenue, from ads for scams and prohibited products. Users saw around 15 billion "higher risk" ads per day, a late-2024 assessment put that category at about $7 billion a year, and Meta's own safety staff estimated the platform facilitated about a third of successful scams in the US. Spokesman Andy Stone says the documents "present a selective view," calls the 10% figure "rough and overly-inclusive," and cites 134 million scam ads removed in 2025 and complaints down 58% over 18 months.

*** Take
Meta's rebuttal disputes the precision of the number, not the existence of the revenue line. A fraud rate at that scale would get a bank's charter pulled; in ad tech it gets a spokesman arguing about methodology.
