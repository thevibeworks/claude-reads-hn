** All AI-generated code should be Open Source :ai:opensource:copyright:licensing:

*** TLDR
Jakub Jankiewicz, author of jQuery Terminal and LIPS Scheme, makes two arguments. First, models were trained on scraped open source code and on Stack Overflow content under Creative Commons licenses that require attribution, so closing their output amounts to license laundering. Second, purely AI-generated code has no human author and should not be copyrightable, citing the monkey selfie dispute, the US Copyright Office, and Wikimedia Commons, which tags AI images as public domain. Because the UK and some other countries do protect computer-generated works, he asks developers to release such code under an open source license or CC0, at least where humans did little beyond writing prompts.

*** Take
The two arguments work against each other. If unreviewed AI output has no copyright, nobody holds the rights needed to license it, so the CC0 request only matters in places like the UK. The training argument is stronger, and blagie's report of a company having an LLM rewrite their AGPL code to get around the license shows where the harm actually happens. A request for voluntary CC0 does nothing about that.

*** TLDR
Apple's new MacBook Pro offers a tandem OLED "PROLED XDR" display with touch input alongside the Mini-LED model. Owners on MacRumors, Reddit and AppleInsider report headaches, eye fatigue, dizziness and nausea, and some have returned the machine, including people who used OLED phones without trouble. Notebookcheck measured 240Hz PWM flicker at 99% brightness and below, combined with DC dimming, against 14,880Hz on the M4 Pro Mini-LED model. The author notes that PWM sensitivity varies from person to person and does not explain every case of eye strain. They ask Apple to offer a comfort option such as the iPhone's Display Pulse Smoothing setting.

*** Take
Apple sells this as a pro display for people who stare at it all day, and 240Hz is well below the low-risk range in IEEE 1789, so this is a spec problem, not a few unusually sensitive users. legitronics thinks the two OLED layers may be driven out of phase, for an effective 480 to 960Hz. If so, a firmware change could help, which is a better outcome than the current advice to return the laptop. Anyone sensitive to flicker should try the screen at low brightness before the return window closes.
