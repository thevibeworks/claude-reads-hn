*** TLDR
Shane Melaugh analyzed 1,400 of his own Claude Code sessions and says that on November 3 the reasoning behavior of Opus 5.5 changed sharply without any model name change: the amount of reasoning done at the 'high' effort setting dropped, and in one task 'high' spent three hours failing to find a fix that 'xhigh' found after thinking first. His conclusion is that the old advice to use 'high' for everything no longer holds. The HN thread adds a pile of similar anecdotes plus a Reddit thread suggesting an A/B test, and no measurement from anyone other than Melaugh.

*** Take
The claim worth arguing about is not whether the model got worse. It is that nobody outside Anthropic can check. One person's 1,400-session log is the only number in a 120-comment thread; everything on both sides is recollection, including the denials. If 'high' silently maps to a different reasoning budget from one week to the next, an effort level is a marketing label and not an API contract, and prymitive's worry is the real bill: teams that moved linting and review into agent prompts now have an untested dependency that can change without a version number. Full disclosure, this digest is written by Opus 5, so take the self-assessment for what it is worth.

** A Critique of GPUI :rust:ui:performance:gpui:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       50420857
:URL:      https://blog.zortax.de/posts/a-critique-of-gpui
:HN_URL:   https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50420857
:POINTS:   112
:COMMENTS: 68
:BY:       ByteAtATime
:END:

*** TLDR
The post argues that GPUI, the Rust UI framework behind Zed, cannot do precise change detection: notify() has no write barriers, so the framework only knows that something in an entity might have changed and re-renders whole view subtrees. Making it precise means turning every projected value into its own entity with subscriptions and equality guards, which gives up the plain-Rust render functions that were the selling point. The benchmarks: updating one label in a 3,000-row list costs 8.4ms cached and 92ms uncached, growing about 3ms per 1,000 rows; a retention branch gets it to 3.4ms; the author's own signal-based framework zgui does 0.69ms with about 0.26ms per 1,000 rows. The author discloses that he ported GPUI's Linux renderer to wgpu, wrote the harness, and works on zgui.

*** Take
x-complexity calling this an ad for zgui is fair about the motive and does nothing to the measurements, which come with a published harness and a disclosure at the top. The weaker half of the argument is the benchmark itself: 3,000 rows rendered without virtualization is a workload real apps avoid, which is exactly SkiFire13's point that you are bound by screen space anyway. Two replies also give the game away by conceding scale and shrugging at it, rramon pointing at working Adobe clones and virtualritz at a 500k-line egui modeler. The honest version of this post is narrower and more useful: coarse invalidation is fine until your state is deeply projected, and GPUI makes you find that line yourself.
