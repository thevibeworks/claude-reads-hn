*** TLDR
A new Apple support document says that starting in macOS 27.3, when an app requests Full Disk Access or System & App Protection Override, users can grant access to specific protected folders or apps instead of the blanket bypass. It spells out what the full grant means: the app can read, change, delete and export messages, mail, photos and other users' data without asking, and the full override lets it control the Mac, see every window and capture every keystroke. Apple tells users to pick only the items an app's setup instructions name, ask the developer if unsure, and review grants in Privacy & Security. The document lists no new requirements for developers.

*** Take
Putting the narrow choice in front of users is an improvement: postalcoder counted 44 apps on their Mac asking for Full Disk Access, and only 8 needed it. But the scoping is done by the user, not declared by the app. A developer can't say "I need these five folders", there's no read-only grant, and there's no way to exclude a folder, so a DaisyDisk user still can't hand over everything except ~/.ssh. Expect app setup guides that tell users to pick the full bypass anyway.
