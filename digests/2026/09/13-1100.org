*** TLDR
JetKVM announced a smaller KVM-over-IP device: 42 x 42 x 23 mm aluminium case, $39 with Ethernet or $42 wireless, dropping to $33 and $36 in three-packs, shipping October 26, 2026. It runs on an ESP32-P4X with a hardware H.264 encoder instead of the original's Linux system, so the separate DRAM and eMMC are gone. Video is 1080p30 or 720p60 over WebRTC, up to 4K with JetKVM OS Services. The wireless Mini W adds an ESP32-C5 for dual-band Wi-Fi and is set up over Bluetooth from a phone. It keeps the same web interface, cloud, extensions, MQTT and Home Assistant support, OIDC login and secure boot.

*** Take
Dropping Linux for a microcontroller is what makes $39 possible, and it should also make the device more reliable, since there is much less to go wrong. That matters here: the top comment is from someone whose three JetKVMs failed in three different ways. A cheaper box from a vendor with a field-failure problem is only a good deal if the rebuild fixed the failures, and the post does not claim it did.

*** TLDR
Homebrew 7.0.0 shipped today. Installs and upgrades are faster because package preparation now overlaps with downloads instead of running after them. Security gets an official advisory database with a new `brew vulns` command, signature verification for third-party tap bottles, and tighter macOS sandboxing that restricts launching applications and opening sockets. On Linux, bubblewrap sandboxing is replaced by Landlock, which removes the external dependency and the Docker permission requirements. BrewUI, a native macOS app that browses and searches packages while showing the underlying terminal commands, is now fully released. Breaking changes: macOS 10.15 support ends now, Intel Macs drop to Tier 3 with no new bottles after September 2027, formula `post_install` blocks are deprecated in favour of declared `⁎_steps` until December 11, 2027, and the Ubuntu 22.04 container image is gone.

*** Take
`brew vulns` is the part worth noticing. Homebrew has been the unmanaged half of most Mac developer machines for years, invisible to whatever scanner the security team runs against the repo, and a first-party advisory database is the cheapest fix for that. The Intel Tier 3 line is the real news for anyone still on a 2019 MacBook: you have until September 2027 before every install starts compiling from source.

*** TLDR
History Today revisits the Anarchy, the 19-year civil war that followed Henry I's death in December 1135. Henry had named his daughter Matilda heir and made his nobles swear allegiance to her, but his cousin Stephen crossed to England and seized the throne anyway. Matilda, backed by her half-brother Robert of Gloucester, gained ground after 1138 and beat and captured Stephen at Lincoln in 1141, yet was never crowned, blocked by hostility to a female ruler that the chroniclers recorded in openly sexist terms. ***** Take
Was sich in jedes Jahrhundert übertragen lässt, ist Wallingford: Ein Krieg endet, weil die, die ihn führen müssen, sich weigern. Beide Anwärter standen noch im Feld und hielten an ihren Ansprüchen fest, und er hörte trotzdem auf. Die zweite These des Artikels ist die schärfere: Gewonnen hat die dynastische Erbfolge, nicht einer der beiden Anwärter. Die Barone entschieden nicht, wer herrschen sollte, sondern wer als Nächster herrschen würde, und das genügte, um die Waffen niederzulegen.
***** Comments
- Vorurteil und fehlende Präzedenz haben Matildas Griff nach der Krone offenkundig erheblich erschwert, aber das spekulative Gedankenspiel vom 33-jährigen Sohn trägt sehr viel Last.
- Eine Frau als Nachfolgerin zu bestimmen war so beispiellos, dass Heinrich seine Barone zum Treueid auf Matilda zwingen musste. Alle leisteten ihn, manche widerwilliger als andere. Dann eilte Stephan nach England und sicherte sich den Schatz.
- Wie wohltuend, auf dem Telefon etwas zu lesen, das wirklich Prosa mit passenden Bildern ist, statt tausender Werbeanzeigen.
