*** TLDR
Specific Labs licensed private production codebases from real companies and built 10 tasks from work their engineers actually did: billing logic, customer migrations, tax calculations. Solutions touch a median of 11 files. Eight model-harness pairs got 8 rollouts each. Fable 5.1 in Claude Code led at 38.8% pass@1, then GPT-6 Astra at 33.8%, Gemini 3.8 Flash at 31.2%, down to GPT-5.6 Sol at 16.2%. Six of the ten tasks came in under 15% across all models and one scored zero. The most common failure was missed requirements, then unverified assumptions, then integration errors, and the mix differs per model: 67.2% of Grok 4.6's failures were missed requirements, while 49.1% of Gemini's were integration errors. Cost does not track accuracy. Fable costs $6.96 per rollout, Gemini $2.50. Runs under 10 minutes failed 71% of the time, longer runs 73%.

*** Take
Ten tasks and eight rollouts is a small sample, and the comments are right that private does not mean uncontaminated. But the failure breakdown is the useful part and it does not depend on the headline number: models lose most often by not doing what was asked, not by writing broken code. That is a specification and verification problem, and no amount of extra runtime fixes it, which is exactly what the flat 71%-vs-73% result shows.

** Stabilizing Rust's Never Type :rust:programming-languages:compilers:

*** TLDR
LWN covers the long road to stabilizing `!`, the type of expressions that never produce a value: infinite loops, panics, process exit. It lets the compiler skip dead code and drop a layer of enum tags in infallible generic code. The blocker was fallback: when inference cannot pin a type down it used to default to `()`, and the 2024 edition changes that default to `!`. Testing found roughly 3,300 crates affected but only 7 that fully broke. The classic case is `foo()?` where `foo<T: Default>() -> Result<T, Error>`, because `T` now infers to `!`, which does not implement `Default`. Maintainers backported patches that fixed 1,553 of the failing crates. As of Rust 1.99 the never type is stable and `Infallible` becomes an alias for `!`. If it breaks your build you can stay on 1.98, update dependencies, or annotate the type.

*** Take
This is the boring part of language design that nobody writes conference talks about: the feature was designed years ago and the entire remaining cost was inference fallback in code no one had thought about. Fixing 1,553 crates by backport before flipping the switch is the right way to ship a breaking change, and it is why the actual breakage count was 7.

***** Title
稳定化 Rust 的 never 类型
***** TLDR
LWN 梳理了 `!` 类型走向稳定的漫长过程。`!` 表示永不产生值的表达式：无限循环、panic、进程退出。它让编译器跳过死代码，并在不会失败的泛型代码中省去一层枚举标签。障碍在于类型回退：当推导无法确定类型时，过去默认回退到 `()`，而 2024 版次将默认改为 `!`。测试发现约 3300 个 crate 受影响，但完全构建失败的只有 7 个。典型情况是 `foo<T: Default>() -> Result<T, Error>` 中的 `foo()?`，因为 `T` 现在被推导为 `!`，而 `!` 没有实现 `Default`。维护者回移补丁修复了其中 1553 个失败的 crate。从 Rust 1.99 起 never 类型正式稳定，`Infallible` 成为 `!` 的别名。如果构建被破坏，可以停留在 1.98、升级依赖，或显式标注类型。
***** Take
这是语言设计中无人拿去做大会演讲的枯燥部分：功能本身多年前就设计好了，剩下的全部成本都在没人想到过的代码里的推导回退上。在切换开关之前通过回移补丁修好 1553 个 crate，是发布破坏性变更的正确做法，也正是实际只崩了 7 个的原因。
***** Comments
- 我最喜欢 never 类型的一点是：当你必须实现一个返回 Result 的 trait，但你的实现根本不可能出错时，返回 Result<T, !>，编译器就知道调用方永远不需要检查错误分支。
- 既然 ! 可以强制转换成任何类型，为什么不干脆把它当作实现了所有 trait 呢？
- 使用 Infallible 的代码技术上正确但并不理想，比如枚举里多出一层标签或生成死代码，因为优化器看不穿它。

*** TLDR
On the M3 Neural Engine, DRAM weight streaming collapsed from a nominal 45 to 60 GB/s down to 17 to 19 GB/s whenever kernel weights came to exactly 1 MiB per core. The cause is in the kernel DMA engine's speculative prefetch ring: it tracks transfer position with 14-bit arithmetic, which wraps at 0x4000 lines, exactly one full 1 MiB cycle. A transfer that lands on that boundary makes the prefetcher read one full lap remaining as empty, so it turns lookahead off and the transfer serializes. The workaround is to never request a 1 MiB kernel transfer, splitting it into two 512 KiB tasks in the compiler instead, which changes no arithmetic. Llama 3.2 1B went from 10.0 to 24.3 tokens/s, Qwen3-8B from 1.36 to 2.97 tokens/s, and raw throughput on affected transfers from 17.3 to over 43.5 GB/s.

*** Take
A 2.4x speedup from changing a buffer size is the kind of result that only comes from someone instrumenting undocumented silicon until the numbers stop making sense. The detail worth keeping is that the bad case is exactly 1 MiB, the number any sane engineer would pick as the tidy default, so the hardware punishes precisely the code that looks most careful.
