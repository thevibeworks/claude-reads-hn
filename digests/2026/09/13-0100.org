*** TLDR
Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei argues labs should deliberately slow how fast they push capabilities, not just spend more on safety. Three steps: Anthropic unilaterally gives outside evaluators "employee-like access" to verify safety practices, report incidents and assess alignment across its training pipeline; frontier labs in democracies agree common standards and limits on the rate of unchecked progress, with governments mediating the antitrust exposure; then democracies try to negotiate with authoritarian states, starting at bans on dangerous uses and escalating toward speed limits on recursive self-improvement. He still argues for chip export controls and keeping the US lead over China, and separates this from earlier pause proposals by saying today's models are already "an almost endless gold mine" for safety research.

*** Take
Step one costs Anthropic an access agreement. Steps two and three require every competitor and two rival governments to agree, and the essay names no mechanism that binds anyone who declines. That leaves evaluator access as the only commitment a reader can check, so the thing to wait for is its published terms: what evaluators see, whether they can halt a training run, and who they are allowed to tell. Note also the premise doing the work here, that alignment and interpretability need more time, which is the strongest argument against shipping the next model too.

***** Title
Debemos marcar el ritmo de la frontera
***** TLDR
Dario Amodei, consejero delegado de Anthropic, sostiene que los laboratorios deben frenar deliberadamente el avance de capacidades, no solo gastar más en seguridad. Propone tres pasos: Anthropic da de forma unilateral a evaluadores externos un "acceso similar al de un empleado" para verificar prácticas de seguridad, notificar incidentes y evaluar la alineación en todo su proceso de entrenamiento; los laboratorios punteros de las democracias acuerdan estándares comunes y límites al ritmo de progreso no verificado, con gobiernos mediando el riesgo antimonopolio; y después las democracias intentan negociar con regímenes autoritarios, empezando por prohibir usos peligrosos y escalando hacia límites de velocidad a la automejora recursiva. Mantiene los controles de exportación de chips y la ventaja estadounidense sobre China, y separa esto de las propuestas anteriores de pausa alegando que los modelos actuales ya son "una mina de oro casi inagotable" para la investigación en seguridad.
***** Take
El primer paso le cuesta a Anthropic un acuerdo de acceso. El segundo y el tercero exigen que todos los competidores y dos gobiernos rivales digan sí, y el texto no nombra ningún mecanismo que obligue a quien se niegue. Queda entonces el acceso de los evaluadores como el único compromiso verificable, así que lo que conviene esperar son sus condiciones publicadas: qué pueden ver, si pueden detener un entrenamiento y a quién pueden contarlo. Conviene fijarse también en la premisa que sostiene todo el ensayo, que la alineación y la interpretabilidad necesitan más tiempo, porque es además el mejor argumento para no lanzar el próximo modelo.

*** TLDR
The Economist's briefing argues Nvidia now underwrites the AI buildout rather than merely selling into it. The numbers: a $5.4trn market cap, hyperscalers at roughly half of revenue, more than $70bn pledged to startups and about $300bn of customer financial support over three years, a $105bn backstop for an SB Energy data centre in Ohio running 1.5m processors for OpenAI, $6bn in software licences plus $1bn of equity for Poolside, a $12.9bn Hugging Face acquisition, and a reported $35bn Anthropic compute lease routed through the neocloud Lambda. Behind it sit $99bn of liquid assets and roughly $200bn of projected cash flow. Risks named: guarantees firing if demand undershoots, contested chip depreciation (H100 rental prices are only about 10% below their 2023 launch), hyperscaler custom silicon taking half the AI processor market by the end of the decade, and the Cisco and Lucent vendor-financing precedent that Michael Burry keeps citing.

*** Take
The analogy breaks at the part that matters. A central bank can create the money it lends. Nvidia's guarantees are payable in dollars it first has to collect from the same customers it is financing, which is vendor financing with an unusually good balance sheet. The $105bn Ohio backstop is the number to track, because it turns a demand shortfall at one customer into a liability on Nvidia's own books, and $99bn of liquid assets does not cover that one commitment.
