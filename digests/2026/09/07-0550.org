*** TLDR
A commit titled "Update README - Nitter lives" reverses the shutdown. The README still records that X Corp sent cease and desist letters on 24 August 2026 demanding permanent takedown of Nitter instances and the repository, but adds: following legal advice, the project will continue, with details to be announced. nitter.net and xcancel.com are serving again. The same commit moves the donation links up into badges and changes the legal contact line to cover DMCA requests.

*** Take
Two weeks ago the news was that people were forking Nitter because the original was dead. The reversal came from a lawyer reading the letter, not from a fork. That is the actual lesson here: a cease and desist is an opening demand, and most maintainers comply because they cannot afford to find out whether it is enforceable. Nitter could. The details X Corp allegedly could not sustain remain unannounced, so hold the celebration until they are.

*** TLDR
OpenAI says it hit its stated goal of an "automated research intern" by September 2026, meaning a system that handles well-defined tasks that would take a skilled researcher several days, and is targeting a fully automated AI researcher by March 2028. The internal numbers are the interesting part: as of mid-August 2026 the median researcher spends over $600/day on API inference, the 90th percentile over $7,000/day, and the research org runs 3.1 agent-workdays per human workday. Experiments per researcher are at an all-time high since tracking began in January 2025. More than half of successful 4-8 hour tasks still needed at least one human intervention.

*** Take
The usage numbers are the credible part because they are things OpenAI can count without judgment: dollars spent, jobs launched, tickets filed. The task success rates are graded by an agentic classifier built by the same company, with uncertain outcomes dropped from the chart, which is a measurement setup no one would accept from a vendor benchmarking a competitor. Read the spend figures and the disappearing internal support office hours; treat the success curve as marketing until someone outside grades it.
