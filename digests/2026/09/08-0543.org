** I've factored the RSA keys of a Certificate Authority from the 90s :security:cryptography:rsa:tls:
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       49604637
:URL:      https://mcpherrin.ca/2026/09/07/rsa.html

:BY:       ahlCVA

*** TLDR
Matthew McPherrin pulled 512-bit root certificates out of archived Netscape 4.51 and Internet Explorer 3.02 installers and factored them with CADO-NFS: 32 hours for the E-Certify RSA 512 Gold Server root on a Ryzen 9 5950X, 29 hours for the client root, and about an hour on a GPU cluster for a VeriSign test code-signing root. He then issued forged certificates under those authorities and wrote a custom TLS server that Netscape 4.51 accepts, running at e-certify.fly.dev. Keys and tools are on GitHub. No modern browser trusts these roots, so the attack is a demonstration.

*** Take
The finding is not that 512-bit RSA is weak, everyone knew that. It is that a commercial CA shipped a key in 1999 with no minimum size rule to stop it, and that key now falls to one desktop over a weekend. The comment asking which governments archived that decade's traffic is the right follow-up question.

*** TLDR
Jellyfin drops the leading 10 from its version number, so 10.12 becomes 12.0, and finishes the database rework started in 10.11. Playlists and collections store each item as its own row instead of loading the whole structure to count or edit it, episodes get alternate versions the way movies already had, book and comic metadata is read from OPF and ComicInfo files without a plugin, ListenBrainz ships as a built-in recommendation source, and the previously experimental UI is now the default. Breaking: the /emby/ and /mediabrowser/ endpoints are removed, plugins must be rebuilt for .NET 10, usernames become case-sensitive so accounts differing only in capitalisation fail migration, and first boot rewrites the schema and forces a full library scan.

*** Take
Renaming 10.12 to 12.0 is the honest move, because the plugin break, the removed legacy endpoints and the forced rescan cost you an evening in every sense that a major version does. Back up the database before you start. The upgrade reports in the thread are good, but they are from people who read the warnings first.
