*** TLDR
The primary document behind the OpenAI Navier-Stokes dispute. On Aug 15 Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge released finite-time blowup results for incompressible porous media, Boussinesq and 3D incompressible Euler, extending work by Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa with help from LLMs. On Sep 6 OpenAI asked for an urgent meeting and said an internal model had produced a roughly 100-page proof of finite-time blowup for forced Navier-Stokes, using the same forcing route he and Alpöge had quietly chosen. He writes that he got no answer when he asked whether his Codex sessions trained their models; that he was told there was very little human input but later learned an entire team had worked on it; and that Sebastien Bubeck twice said he wanted Alpöge removed from authorship because Alpöge works at Anthropic. He declined both a simultaneous post and a paper crediting OpenAI's model, and was asked, "Why would you ruin your career?" He says he is not accusing anyone, only recording what he was told and when.

*** Take
The timing of the proof is arguable; the authorship demand is not. Asking a mathematician to remove his coauthor because that coauthor works for a competitor has nothing to do with who proved what, and that is the one detail nobody at OpenAI has denied.

*** TLDR
Inception Labs' new diffusion language model, which it calls the largest diffusion LM ever trained. It runs at 1,107 tokens per second on widely available NVIDIA GPUs, takes 260K context, and claims a 40% intelligence gain over Mercury 2, putting it alongside cost-optimized models like GPT-5.6 Luna (Low), Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite and Claude Haiku 4.5. New in this release: tunable reasoning, parallel tool calls and schema-aligned JSON. Pricing is $0.20 per million input tokens and $0.75 per million output, discounted 80% at launch to $0.04 and $0.15. Served through the Inception API, Baseten and OpenRouter. Weights are not released.

*** Take
The speed number is the product and the comparison set gives away the tier: cost-optimized models, not frontier ones. Diffusion decoding earning 1,107 tok/s at Haiku-class quality is a real result for latency-bound work like autocomplete and agent loops. With closed weights, nobody outside can check whether the quality claim holds past the benchmark table.
