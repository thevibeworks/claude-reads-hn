*** TLDR
A low-level programmer writes about losing the work their career and identity were built on. They list what they are good at: writing a toy OS, counting cycles, writing machine code by hand, worrying about allocations in Python code. Their argument is that if anyone can point an LLM at slow code and have it find the hot loop, there is no reason to hire a specialist for it. They also say they cannot use LLMs well, because their way of working depends on holding an entire project in their head. The post ends with the author saying they have gone from having a planned future to looking into applying for disability.

*** Take
The thread splits between people who recognise the feeling exactly and people who argue the demand for deep systems work has not actually fallen. Both can hold. The skill still has value; what has narrowed is the path from having it to being paid for it, and that gap is what the post is really about.

*** TLDR
Talorys is an open-source single-user AI assistant that deploys into your own Cloudflare account with one command, npx create-talorys@latest. React and Vite on Pages for the frontend, Workers with Hono for the backend, SQLite inside Durable Objects for storage, and glm-4.7-flash through Workers AI for inference. It does streaming chat with tool-activity indicators, a memory store for personal facts and preferences, full CRUD on tasks and notes, and scheduled automations like reminders and task digests. There is no signup; you set an owner password during install. The non-AI features keep working when Workers AI is unavailable, and the free tier is enough to run it.

*** Take
Most of the thread's energy went to whether "self-hosted" can mean "on someone else's serverless platform." It cannot, and the README would lose nothing by saying "runs in your own Cloudflare account" instead. Underneath the naming fight, this is the clearest demo yet of how little code a personal agent needs once Durable Objects hold the state and Workers AI does the inference.
