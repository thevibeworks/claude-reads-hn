A Max Planck Institute study found that Italian and Dutch adults adapt their hand gestures identically when teaching children, despite Italians using more gestures overall. Both groups switch to more two-handed, visually rich gestures when explaining concepts to kids, suggesting humans share deep-rooted pedagogical instincts across cultures.
