Valkey 9.2, now in release-candidate testing, replaces the skiplist behind large sorted sets with fbtree, a B+ tree with 61-way fanout and 512-byte leaves sized to jemalloc classes. Score and member are packed into one value, inner nodes keep a 4-byte 'feature' per child that SIMD compares in parallel, and leaves are doubly linked for range scans. Each skiplist node cost about 37 bytes before the member string. With 5M 20-byte members, per-member index overhead fell from 50.3B to 28.5B for sequential inserts (43%) and to 32.0B for random inserts (36%). On a 3M-member set, ZADD score updates got 105% faster, ZREM 76%, ZCOUNT 27% and ZRANK 19%; hashtable-backed commands like ZSCORE barely moved. Commands are unchanged, but OBJECT ENCODING now reports 'btree' instead of 'skiplist'. Small listpack sets are unaffected.

The method write-up is careful about uncertainty: it reports a range instead of a frequency and builds in the octave check. The comments show the limits of single-pitch matching. Many people hear several tones, different tones in each ear, or pitches above the original 12 kHz slider cap, which brianrhea raised to 16 kHz during the thread. brianrhea says the research on notching is mixed. The most repeated advice in the thread was to see a doctor first to rule out physical causes; one commenter's tinnitus turned out to be earwax against the eardrum.
