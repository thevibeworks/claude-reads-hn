It dates to about 1820 to 1830 and was probably made in Bristol, a glassmaking centre. It has been broken and repaired, has likely lost its lid, and may be one of only two known glass examples, the other at the British Museum. Bowls like this belonged to the sugar boycotts of 1792 to 1793 and 1825 to 1829, led by Quakers and abolitionists such as William Fox, whose pamphlet compared eating slave-grown sugar to cannibalism.
